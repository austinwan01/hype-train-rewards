Product concept · Mobile Hype Train

Creator Rewards on the Hype Train

Streamers attach their own challenges to Hype Train levels. When chat pushes the train to level 5, the streamer has to drop every gun in their Fortnite loadout. Viewers on mobile can see what is coming next and what they have already unlocked.

Creator sets it upA new Rewards tab in the Hype Train settings of the creator dashboard. One reward per level.
Viewers chase itThe HypeBar gains a "next reward" line, and the HypePanel gains a Streamer Rewards section.
Streamer pays it offUnlocked rewards land in a queue. The streamer or a mod taps Done, and chat gets a NoticeBar callout.

Mockups are built from the Midnight Rail design system: HypeBar, HypePanel, RemainingCard, UnlockCard, InfoCard, ActionRow, ChatMessage, NoticeBar and ChatInputBar, with its color, spacing and type tokens. New pieces for this feature are marked "new" in the captions.

Why this matters

Hype Train rewards today are emotes and badges set by the platform, and they are the same on every channel. The levels feel like a progress bar more than a moment. Letting creators add their own payoffs turns each level into something chat can see happen on stream, which is the part viewers actually rally around.

Mobile is where the gap is widest. The HypeBar shows a level, a timer and a percentage, and nothing about why the next level is worth it. This concept gives that bar a goal to point at.

Goals

  • Creators can add, edit and reorder custom rewards per level in under a minute.
  • Mobile viewers always see the next reward and how close the train is to it.
  • Streamers can see what they owe and clear it without leaving the game for long.

Out of scope for v1

  • Rewards that trigger automatically through game integrations or Extensions.
  • Viewer voting on which reward to attach to which level.
  • Rewards with real-world costs such as giveaways or merch (those go through existing giveaway rules).

1. Setting up rewards in the creator dashboard

A new Rewards tab sits next to the existing Hype Train settings (difficulty, cooldown, contribution types). It shows the level track as a list. Each row is a level, and a level without a custom reward still shows the default emote reward so the creator knows nothing is lost by leaving it empty.

Creators can also save reward sets per category (one for Fortnite, one for Just Chatting), and the right set switches on when they change category. Editing is locked while a train is running so a reward can't be swapped out after chat has started paying for it.

2. What viewers see on mobile

The HypeBar keeps its size and layout. Under it, a slim line names the next reward. Expanding the bar opens the HypePanel, where a new Streamer Rewards section sits between the RemainingCard and Unlockable Emotes, and the UnlockCard sells the next reward instead of the next emote. When a level is reached, an UnlockCard rises over the video and a NoticeBar pins the reward in chat.

Viewer details

  • If the channel has no custom rewards, the HypeBar and HypePanel look exactly like today.
  • When several levels unlock in one big gift bomb, UnlockCards queue and each shows for 3 seconds instead of 6.
  • After the train ends, the Streamer Rewards section stays reachable from the channel page for the rest of the stream as "Rewards owed", so late viewers can see what is coming.
  • Push notifications are opt-in: "Notify me when DropZoneDana does a Hype Train reward" helps viewers catch the payoff live.

3. How the streamer marks a reward as done

Unlocked rewards go into a Rewards queue. It lives in three places so the streamer never has to tab out of the game: the Stream Manager panel on desktop, the Twitch Studio dock, and the Stream Manager tab in the mobile app. Mods can mark rewards done too, which is how most streamers mid-game will actually use it.

Reward lifecycle

Draft→In review→Live→Unlocked→DoneorOverdueorSkipped
  • Overdue: the due window passed without a Done tap. The reward row turns rank-3 copper, viewers see "Owed since last stream", and the streamer gets a reminder at the start of the next stream.
  • Skipped: the streamer can skip with a reason chosen from a short list (game ended, technical issue, unsafe). Skips are shown to viewers honestly as "Skipped: game ended".
  • A creator's public completion rate (for example "18 of 19 rewards done") is shown in the Streamer Rewards section once they have 5 or more unlocks. It gives viewers a reason to trust the promise.

Design system changes

Most of the feature reuses Midnight Rail components as they are. These are the additions it needs:

ComponentChange
HypeBarNew optional nextReward slot under the collapsed bar (surface-2, rank-1 level label).
RewardRowNew component with status=locked | next | owed | done | overdue, built on surface-2 with a 30pt level tile.
UnlockCardCopy variant for a creator reward. Also used as the over-video unlock toast and the queue's top item.
NoticeBarNew type=hype variant (accent-hype tint and top border) for owed rewards, and a trailing button slot for "Clip it".
GiftTierCardUsed as is. Only addition is a selected state: a 2pt rank-1 ring outside the card.
RewardBannerNew header for the gift and Bits sheets: reward name plus a bar showing current progress and how much the selected gift adds.
RewardDetail sheetNew BottomSheet content: level tile, title, creator quote, detail list, notify toggle, primary CTA and report link.
TokensNo new colors. Rank-1 gold means "next", accent-hype means "unlocked", rank-3 copper means "overdue".

Edge cases

SituationBehaviour
Train hits level 5, then the match ends before guns are droppedReward rolls to the next match automatically, since "Next match" means the next one the streamer can play.
Streamer changes game mid-trainThe train keeps the reward set it started with. The new set applies from the next train.
Stream ends with rewards owedThey move to "Next stream" and show on the channel page until done or skipped.
Reward edited while a train is runningEditing is locked during a train and for 5 minutes after it ends.
A reward is unsafe or breaks guidelinesBlocked at review. Viewers can report a reward from the HypePanel, and Trust & Safety can remove it live.
Train beats the all-time recordLevels without a custom reward get the default emote reward, same as today.

How we'd know it works

  • Hype Train average peak level on channels with custom rewards versus a matched control.
  • HypeBar expand rate on channels with custom rewards.
  • Share of unlocked rewards marked done within their due window (target 85% or higher).
  • Clips created within 2 minutes of a "reward done" event.
  • Reports per 1,000 rewards, to watch for unsafe or misleading challenges.

Open questions

  1. Should mods be able to add or edit rewards, or only mark them done?
  2. Do we let creators put a reward on every level from day one, or cap v1 at 5 custom rewards to keep review volume down?
  3. Is "Push to next stream" once per reward too generous, or too strict?
  4. Should the completion rate be public, or visible only to the creator at first?
